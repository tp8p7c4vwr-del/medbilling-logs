Records, retention and backups

Your records live only on this device. Keeping, backing up and retaining them (at least 10 years from the last entry, longer for minors and obstetric records, and any longer legal or College requirement) is your responsibility. The developer has no copy and cannot recover your data or your passcode.

Retention in this app: entries and their audit records are kept for at least 10 years from the last entry (CPSA standard; CMPA recommends at least 10 years). For an entry flagged minor, the longer of 10 years or 2 years after the patient turns 18 (CPSA). For an entry flagged obstetric, 10 years after the infant reaches the age of majority (CMPA), counted conservatively as 29 years from the last entry. After that, records become eligible for removal, but nothing is ever deleted automatically: you review and confirm. A longer legal or College requirement always takes precedence.

Last backup: never

Audit log

Every create, edit and delete of an encounter, arrival/departure or call-back is recorded, append-only and encrypted. Each record carries the hash of the previous one, so any change, removal or reordering is detected.

Delete all data

Removes every encounter, arrival/departure, call-back, photo, the audit log, settings and the passcode from this device. This cannot be undone; export a backup first if you want a copy.

Privacy

Everything is stored only on this device, encrypted with AES-GCM using a key derived from your passcode (PBKDF2-SHA-256, 600,000 iterations). Photos are kept inside the app's encrypted store, never in your camera roll. There are no accounts, no analytics and no third-party scripts, and the app sends nothing anywhere. Files leave the device only when you share or download them.

Encrypted exports. Every report and audit-log export is an AES-256 password-protected .zip, protected by an export password you choose (different from your passcode, never stored). Exports contain health information: send the password separately, never in the same email or message, and store and send the file only as permitted by Alberta's Health Information Act (HIA), your custodian's or AHS privacy and information-security policies, and the privacy law and privacy commissioner's guidance in your province or territory (e.g. Alberta OIPC). Delete copies you no longer need.

Your responsibility. As the custodian or an affiliate of a custodian, you are responsible for handling this information in a compliant way. The app is designed to support compliance and does not transmit exports anywhere by itself; it is not certified or approved by AHS or any regulator.

    1. Getting started, passcode and easy unlock

    Add it to your Home Screen first

    Install the app before you create a passcode, then always open it from the Home Screen icon. It then opens full screen and works offline.

    • iPhone / iPad (Safari): open the app's web address, tap Share (square with an arrow), then Add to Home Screen, then Add.
    • Android (Chrome): open the web address, tap the ⋮ menu, then Install app or Add to Home screen.

    On iPhone, the Home Screen app keeps its own storage, separate from Safari. Entries made in a Safari tab do not appear in the Home Screen app, so pick one and stick with it (the Home Screen app is best).

    First launch: passcode or passphrase

    • Choose Passcode (numbers), at least 6 digits (8 or more is safer), or Passphrase (words), at least 12 characters, where words and spaces are allowed (for example four unrelated words). Type it twice. Both make the encryption key the same strong way (PBKDF2-SHA-256, 600,000 rounds); a longer passphrase is much harder to guess.
    • Tick the two boxes: you understand a forgotten passcode can't be recovered, and you accept responsibility for keeping and backing up your records.
    • Your passcode is never stored anywhere. It makes the key that encrypts your data.
    • To change it later, or to switch between a passcode and a passphrase: Settings → Lock → Change passcode / passphrase. Everything is re-encrypted with the new one.

    Easy unlock: Face ID, Touch ID, fingerprint, passkey or quick PIN

    Typing the passcode every time is optional. Turn on easy unlock in Settings → Lock → Easy unlock (you type your passcode or passphrase once to turn it on). It is off until you choose it.

    • iPhone / iPad app: Unlock with Face ID (or Touch ID). A copy of the encryption key is wrapped with a key kept in the iOS Keychain that only opens after Face ID / Touch ID, and stops working if the enrolled faces or fingers change.
    • Android app: Unlock with fingerprint (or face unlock where the phone offers strong biometrics), using the Android Keystore the same way.
    • Quick PIN (iPhone, iPad and Android app only): a 4 to 6 digit PIN. It is stretched with the same strong key derivation as the passcode and combined with a secret held in the device Keychain / Keystore. After 5 wrong PINs the quick PIN is erased and you need the full passcode. It is not offered in the browser, because a short PIN stored in a browser could be guessed offline.
    • Browser / Home Screen web app: Unlock with a passkey (Face ID, Touch ID, Windows Hello or a phone/security key) where the browser and passkey support the PRF extension: Safari 18 or later on iPhone, iPad (18.4 or later is most reliable) and Mac (macOS 15 or later) with iCloud Keychain passkeys; Chrome on Android with Google Password Manager; Chrome or Edge on Mac; on Windows, Windows Hello works only on Windows 11 25H2 with the February 2026 update and Chrome/Edge 147 or later (or Firefox 148 or later). Where the browser lacks it the option is hidden; if a particular passkey can’t do it, the app says so and leaves easy unlock off.
    • Your passcode or passphrase always works and is never replaced. Changing it turns easy unlock off until you turn it on again.
    • Easy unlock never opens exports. Export passwords are always typed and must differ from your passcode.
    • Your data stays encrypted the same way (AES-GCM with a 600,000-round key). Easy unlock only stores an extra wrapped copy of the key that the device's biometrics, passkey or PIN can open.
    • Readable automatic backups that use "same as the app passcode" need one unlock with the passcode itself in that session.

    If you forget the passcode

    No one can recover it or open your data, including the developer. The only way back in is Forgot passcode? on the lock screen: type DELETE to erase everything on this device and start again. If you have an encrypted backup and remember that backup's passcode, you can import it afterwards. This is why backups matter.

    After 5 wrong passcodes in a row, the app makes you wait (30 seconds at first, longer with each further miss, up to 15 minutes).

    The screens

    • Today: one slim toolbar (date, facility, Arrive/Depart, week totals, + Call-back, Report / share and ⋯ More) above the encounter spreadsheet. Running rows count up live in the Min column; a totals row is pinned at the bottom.
    • History: the same spreadsheet grouped by day, with week totals, timeline, review and reports.
    • Resources: official billing, privacy and regulatory websites, including MedBilling Fee Desk.
    • Settings: defaults, timer warnings, time periods and holidays, lock, backups, retention, audit log, this manual and Delete all data.

    2. The time-period bar

    Per user, verify against the current SOMB. These periods were set up from your own list. MedBilling Logs doesn't attach fee codes to them. Check the current Alberta Schedule of Medical Benefits before you bill.

    The bar under the header shows the billing time period you're in now (America/Edmonton time):

    • The period name and hours, and a progress bar through the period.
    • The time left until the next period starts, and the next period's name.
    • The period's total units, the units elapsed so far, and the units you've logged in encounters during this period. Units are 15-minute blocks.

    • Statutory holidays bill like weekends. Alberta's general holidays are worked out automatically each year: New Year's Day, Alberta Family Day, Good Friday, Victoria Day, Canada Day, Heritage Day (optional in Alberta), Labour Day, National Day for Truth and Reconciliation, Thanksgiving, Remembrance Day and Christmas. Switch any of them off under Settings → Time periods and holidays.
    • For any other holiday, or an observed day (for example when a holiday falls on a weekend), tick Today is a statutory holiday in Settings on that day.

    3. Arrival, departure and call-backs

    Time on site

    1. On Today, tap Facility and pick your hospital (Alberta hospitals, grouped by AHS zone; type to filter). For a clinic or a site not listed, type a custom name under Other / clinic.
    2. Tap Arrive when you get there. The button changes to Depart; tap it when you leave.
    3. Forgot to tap Arrive? Open ⋯ More → Edit arrival time / back-date to set the real time.

    "Time on site today" adds up all your on-site periods for the day.

    Call-backs

    1. Tap + Call-back. Choose Return to hospital or Phone call-back, and set Time called (the time you were contacted).
    2. Save. The call-back timer starts running from now (or set an earlier arrival time with the quick buttons).
    3. Tap Stop when you finish. Under Linked encounters, tick the encounters seen during that call-back.

    Call-backs show a CB badge and have their own totals.

    4. Encounters: the spreadsheet

    Today is a spreadsheet, like Excel or Google Sheets: gridlines, a grey header row and row numbers, a sticky header, and a totals row (encounters, minutes, units) pinned at the bottom. Columns: # · Patient name · MRN / PHN · H/C (Hospital or Clinic) · In · Out · Min · Units · Fee code(s) · Dx (ICD-9) · Billing notes · ⋯. There are always at least 15 blank rows below your entries. Every change saves encrypted straight away; there are no pop-up forms for normal work.

    1. New patient: type a name or MRN/PHN in the first blank row. That creates the encounter (not started yet).
    2. Start the clock: type the time in In (24-hour: 0930, 9:30, 21h05; type n for now), or tap ▶ in the In cell. The row's Min cell then counts up live.
    3. Stop: type the time in Out (an Out earlier than In means after midnight), or tap ■ in the Out cell.
    4. H/C: tap (or press Space, or type H or C) to switch between Hospital and Clinic.
    5. Codes: type or paste fee and ICD-9 codes (several separated by commas). Hover or select the cell and tap ↗ to pick in MedBilling Fee Desk; the code comes back through a same-device handoff, never in the URL.

    Columns fit what you type

    Each column widens to fit its longest entry (and narrows again when you shorten it). Past a sensible maximum, Patient name, Fee code(s), Dx and Billing notes wrap onto more lines and the row grows taller, so nothing is cut off. On a narrow screen the grid scrolls sideways; # and Patient name stay pinned on the left.

    Moving between cells: only when you say so

    The cursor never jumps to the next cell on its own, not after a full PHN, a 4-digit time or a fee code, and not when a save finishes. Rows also stay where they are while you work; they are put back in time order when you leave the grid. You move to another cell only with:

    • Tab or Enter: next cell to the right (after Billing notes, the first cell of the next row). Shift-Tab or Shift-Enter: previous cell.
    • The ← → buttons in the toolbar (same as Shift-Tab / Tab).
    • Clicking or tapping a cell.
    • ↑ ↓ move up or down a row only right after you arrive in a cell (before you start typing). ← → arrow keys always move the cursor inside the text and never leave the cell.
    • Esc cancels the edit. Typing over a selected cell replaces it; click, or press F2, to edit inside it.
    • On a computer, Shift-Enter inside Billing notes starts a new line.

    On a phone or tablet

    • Tap a cell to type in it; tap another cell to move there. Nothing moves while you type.
    • The keyboard's Next / return key moves to the next cell, in every column (including Billing notes, where return never adds a new line on the phone).
    • While a cell is active, a small ← Next → bar floats above the keyboard; the cell you are in is scrolled into view above the keyboard. Text is 16 px, so iPhone doesn't zoom in.

    The ⋯ row menu

    Tap ⋯ at the end of a row (or press and hold the row number; right-click on a computer) for: Start now, Pause, Resume, Stop, Add time, Segments & photos, Add note, Same patient (new encounter), pick fee or dx in Fee Desk, Full-screen timer, Open details and Delete. Double-click a row number to open the details.

    • Next unit: hover the Units cell of a running row to see when the next unit starts. A running row turns amber after 60 minutes and red after 3 hours (Settings → Timers). This is a display only: recorded minutes are always the real minutes, never rounded.
    • Undo: after Delete, an Undo button shows for 5 seconds. Both the action and the undo are kept in the audit log.
    • Forgotten timer: type the real time in Out. If a timer is red when you unlock, the app asks: Stop now, Stop at time or Keep running. A corrected time is saved as "entered later" with a note in the audit log. All of this happens inside the app, with no notifications.
    • Full-screen timer: big digits for procedures, readable from a distance (⋯ → Full-screen timer). It shows the time only, plus Pause and Stop. Auto-lock still applies, so for long procedures set a longer auto-lock first.
    • Other days: use ‹ › or tap the date in the toolbar to show another day in the same grid; Today jumps back. Rows typed on a past day are marked "entered later".

    Open details (⋯ → Open details) for segments, photos and multi-code search (section 5). Patient name and MRN/PHN never go into URLs or console logs.

    • In the details editor, the quick buttons (now, 5, 15, 30 min ago, 1 h ago, End now) fix start or end times in one tap. + Add time segment adds another stretch.
    • If times overlap another entry, you'll see a warning. Check the times, or tap Save anyway if it's correct (for example, two patients seen together).
    • Timers use clock times, so they keep counting when the app is locked, in the background, or the phone is off.
    • Units are shown as a guide: full 15-minute blocks, plus 1 if the remainder is 8 minutes or more (38 min = 3 units). Always confirm time-based billing rules in the Alberta SOMB.
    • On a phone the grid scrolls sideways; the row number and patient name stay pinned on the left.

    5. Entry details: add time, same patient, notes

    Tap any encounter, call-back or on-site entry to open its details. A row of three buttons sits at the top:

    • + Add time: tap +5, +15 or +30 min. On a finished or paused entry the end moves later (never past now; any remainder moves the start earlier). On a running timer the start moves earlier. The change is saved right away, marked "entered later" and kept in the audit log, and Undo appears beside it. New segment (start / end)… opens the editor with a new time segment to fill in.
    • ↔ Same patient, new encounter: starts a new timer for the same patient. The room/label, initials, chart/MRN, setting, facility and the Minor/Obstetric flags are carried over. Fee codes, diagnostic codes, notes and photos are left blank. If the first encounter is still running, it stops first. The two entries are linked: each shows a Same patient line in its details (tap it to jump to the other), History rows show ↔, and the CSV report has a same_patient column. This is different from Track again, which copies only the codes and never patient details.
    • ✎ Edit: opens the full editor. Every field can be changed: start and end times and segments, room/label, initials, chart/MRN, setting, type, facility, fee codes, diagnostic codes, Minor/Obstetric flags, call-back type and time called, linked encounters and photos. Save records the before and after in the audit log. Cancel returns to the details without changes.

    Below the buttons is a summary of the entry: date and times, minutes, units and time periods, patient details, setting and facility, codes with diagnostic codes, retention, same-patient links and photos.

    Notes. An entry can have several notes, each with its own date and time.

    • Tap + Add note, type up to 1000 characters, and tap Save note. Notes save straight away, encrypted with the entry.
    • Each note has Edit and Delete. Edited notes show "(edited HH:MM)". A delete asks first. Every add, edit and delete is kept in the audit log, so a deleted note can still be found in the entry's history.
    • The Billing notes column is a separate one-line note that goes into reports. A ✎ on a row means the entry also has dated notes.
    • Notes from earlier versions (the single short note) were moved into the first note automatically.
    • Notes are left out of reports unless you tick Include notes when you create the report (section 12).

    Quick actions from a row. On Today or History, tap ⋯ at the end of a row, or press and hold the row number (right-click on a computer), to get Add time, Add note, Same patient, new encounter, Open details and more.

    6. Track again and code sets

    • The Track again chips (Today → ⋯ More) show your recent and favourite combinations, for example 03.03A · 780. Tap one to start a timer with that setting, facility, fee code(s) and ICD-9 code(s) already filled in.
    • Tap ☆ on a chip to save it as a favourite code set (★). Favourites come first. Tap ★ to remove it.
    • In any saved encounter's details, Track again (codes only) starts a new timer with the same codes, and ☆ Save code set saves them as a favourite.
    • Only billing fields are copied. Room labels, initials, chart numbers, notes and photos are never copied. To carry the patient over instead (with blank codes), use Same patient, new encounter (section 5).

    7. Past entries and "Entered later"

    For a quick past entry, go to the day with ‹ › in the toolbar and type the row with its In and Out times. Or use ⋯ More → + Add past entry on Today for something you didn't time live, or tap an empty spot in the day timeline. Set the start and end, add details, and save.

    • Last stop sets the start to when your previous entry today ended, so back-to-back patients have no gaps.
    • Start −5 / +5 and End −5 / +5 nudge the times by 5 minutes. Length +15 / +30 extends the end. Times can't go into the future.
    • The entry shows its time-period split as you change the times.

    To keep the record honest, the app marks an entry Entered later when:

    • a new entry starts more than 2 minutes before it was saved (back-dated), or
    • you change the times of an existing entry, including with + Add time.

    The badge shows when it was created and last edited. It also appears in reports (the entered_later and last_edited columns) and in the audit log. It isn't an error. It shows the times were entered afterwards rather than timed live.

    8. Billing and diagnostic codes

    In the spreadsheet, type or paste a fee or ICD-9 code into the cell, or tap ↗ Pick in Fee Desk. Fee Desk writes the chosen code back when you return (shared local handoff on this device; no patient data leaves the vault).

    1. Open an encounter. Under Fee codes and diagnostic codes, check the fee schedule (province) on the right.
    2. In Billing code, type a code or a few words and tap a suggestion to add it.
    3. In Diagnostic code (ICD-9) beside it, type a code or words and tap a suggestion. Each billing code you add gets its own diagnostic code field on the same row. You can also type any code that isn't in the list.
    • Suggestions come from lists built into the app (fee codes from MedBilling Fee Desk; ICD-9 from Alberta Health's diagnostic code supplement). They work offline and nothing you type is sent anywhere.
    • Look up in Fee Desk opens MedBilling Fee Desk in a new tab with fees, rules and notes. It goes straight to the code only if what you typed is a known code. Otherwise it opens Fee Desk's home page. No patient information is ever put in the link.
    • Opening Fee Desk locks this app (it went to the background). Your unsaved entry is kept as an encrypted draft and reopens after you unlock.
    • Nova Scotia (approval pending): code lookup is unavailable until permission is granted, so no Nova Scotia code list is included. Type the code manually (it is saved exactly as typed) and confirm it in the official MSI Physician’s Manual. Codes already saved in your entries are kept.
    • Codes and fees are a convenience. Always confirm in the official Schedule of Medical Benefits before you submit claims.

    9. Photos and the lock

    • In an encounter, tap Take photo or Attach photo. Photos are resized, location data is removed, and they are stored only inside the app, encrypted. They aren't saved to your camera roll.
    • Tap a thumbnail to view it or Delete photo.
    • Move clinical photos into the official patient record, then delete them here.
    • The lock: the app locks whenever it goes to the background and after a period without use (Settings → Lock; 1 to 15 minutes, default 2). Tap Lock at the top right to lock right away. The camera doesn't trigger a lock while you take a photo.

    10. History, week strip and timeline

    • History shows every day as the same spreadsheet as Today, and you can edit cells there too. Open in Today shows that day in the Today grid.
    • Rows are grouped by day (newest first) and by week. Each day shows its totals: number of encounters, Hospital minutes/units (H), Clinic minutes/units (C), call-backs (CB) and time on site.
    • Double-click a row number to open the details. Tap ⋯ (or press and hold the row number) for quick actions: Add time, Add note, Same patient, Edit. In an entry, History of this entry shows every change made to it.
    • Each day also shows its time periods (for example Eve 45m/3u · Late 20m/1u).
    • Week strip (top of History; Today has a compact date control with ‹ › and Today): Monday to Sunday with minutes and units under each day. Today is outlined, holidays are tinted, and ✓ marks reviewed days. Tap ‹ › or swipe to change weeks, and tap a day to jump to it. Tap Today (it turns teal when you're looking at another week) to go straight back to the current week; in History it also scrolls to today's entries. Today and This week totals sit beside it. On Today, the totals row at the bottom of the grid splits the day by Hospital, Clinic and time period.
    • Timeline (Today, or a day in History) draws the day as blocks: on-site time as a shaded band, Hospital in teal, Clinic in orange, call-backs dashed. Gaps and overlaps are easy to see. Tap a block to open it, or tap an empty spot to add a past entry at that time.

    11. Review a day or week

    Before you submit claims, tap ⋯ More → Review day on Today, or Review / Review week in History. The checklist shows:

    • Fix: timers still running or paused, and arrivals with no departure. These must be cleared before you can mark the period reviewed.
    • Check: entries with no fee code or no diagnostic code, and overlapping entries.
    • Info: entries marked "entered later".

    Tap an item to open and fix it. Then tap Mark reviewed. The day shows ✎ Reviewed and the audit log records it. If any entry on that day changes later, the mark is removed, the day shows Edited after review, and the audit log records that too.

    12. Encrypted reports (PDF, Word, Excel, CSV) and sharing safely

    Every export is encrypted. Reports and the audit log are always delivered inside an AES-256 password-protected .zip (WinZip AES format). There is no unencrypted export.

    1. On Today tap Report / share, or on History pick From and To dates and tap Create report.
    2. Choose PDF, Word, Excel or CSV. For PDF and Word, tick Include photos only if you need them. Include notes is off by default; tick it only if the recipient needs your notes.
    3. Tap Create encrypted file…. Read the privacy notice and tick I understand (the tick is remembered until the app locks).
    4. Type an export password twice. Tap Show to check what you typed. A live meter shows its strength and says plainly why a password is refused.
    5. Tap Encrypt file, then Share… or Download. The file is named like MedBillingLogs_2026-10-06.zip. Neither the file name nor the share text ever includes patient details or the password.

    Export password rules

    • You choose it, and it must be different from your app passcode or passphrase. The app checks this on the device, in memory only.
    • At least 12 characters with 3 of these 4: lowercase, UPPERCASE, numbers, symbols. Or a passphrase of 16+ characters (several unrelated words).
    • Refused: common or leaked-style passwords (like Password2026! or a city or team name), sequences (abcd, 1234), keyboard patterns (qwerty), repeated characters (aaa), and anything containing a patient's name or MRN/PHN.
    • It is never stored. You type it for each export. If you tick Remember it for this session only, it stays in memory until the app locks, then it is cleared. Face ID, Touch ID, passkeys and the quick PIN never unlock exports.
    • If it is lost, no one can open the file, including the developer. Zip encryption uses a weaker key-stretching step than the app itself, so a long password matters.

    Sending it

    Send the .zip and its password separately: never in the same email or message (for example, send the file through your clinic's secure system and give the password by phone). Use only channels your custodian approves, and delete copies (Downloads, email attachments, Files) you no longer need. The app itself never sends exports anywhere.

    Opening the encrypted .zip

    The recipient needs an app that supports AES-256 zip. The built-in tools on iPhone, Mac and Windows don't:

    • iPhone / iPad: the Files app can unzip ordinary zips but can't open AES-256 encrypted zips. Install the free iZip app (App Store; it says it opens "password protected and AES encrypted ZIP files"; the free version shows ads). In Files, touch and hold the .zip, tap Share, choose iZip, then type the password.
    • Mac: double-clicking (Archive Utility) can't open AES-256 zips. Use the free The Unarchiver (Mac App Store or theunarchiver.com) or Keka (keka.io): open the .zip with it and type the password. In Terminal: tar -xf FILE.zip --passphrase 'PASSWORD'.
    • Windows: File Explorer's built-in zip support doesn't open encrypted archives (Microsoft recommends a tool like 7-Zip). Install the free 7-Zip (7-zip.org), right-click the .zip, choose 7-Zip → Extract Here and type the password.
    • Android: use an archive app that supports AES zip.

    Inside is the report (PDF, Word, Excel or CSV) with its usual contents: each entry with times, minutes, units, facility, patient name and MRN/PHN, billing and diagnostic codes and time periods, plus daily and overall totals in PDF, Word and Excel. Once unzipped, the report itself is not encrypted: open it on a secure device and delete the unzipped copy when you're done. The names of the files inside the zip are visible without the password, so they never contain patient details.

    13. Audit log

    • Every create, edit, delete, start/pause/stop, undo, arrival/departure and import is recorded with its time and the before/after values. So are added time, same-patient links, each note added, edited or deleted, review marks (and their removal), favourite code sets, holiday settings, backup settings, backups made with Back up now, and automatic backup failures and recoveries (never the backup password). The log is encrypted and append-only.
    • Each record is chained to the one before it, so any change, removal or reordering is detected.
    • Settings → Audit log: Check log verifies the chain, Deleted entries lists what was deleted, Export log saves it as PDF or CSV (note text is left out unless you tick Include notes).

    14. Retention and backups

    How long records are kept

    • At least 10 years from the last entry (CPSA; CMPA recommends at least 10 years).
    • Minor flag: the longer of 10 years or 2 years after the patient turns 18.
    • Obstetric flag: 10 years after the infant reaches majority (counted as 29 years).
    • Nothing is ever deleted automatically. Settings → Records past retention shows what is eligible, and you confirm before anything is removed. Any longer legal or College requirement comes first.

    Backups

    • Your records exist only on this device. If the phone is lost, broken or reset, or the app or its website data is deleted, they're gone unless you have a backup.
    • Automatic backups (off until you turn them on) save a backup every hour or at the interval you choose: see section 15.
    • Settings → Encrypted backup (manual export and import) → Export, confirm your passcode, then save the file (medbilling-logs-backup-DATE.mblbackup) somewhere safe that only you control. It's encrypted with your current passcode.
    • Import loads a backup (you need the passcode it was made with). It's added to what's already there. Use it to move to a new phone.
    • Under every backup button (Back up now in Settings → Backups, the reminder banner on Today, and Export) a short line says how the file is encrypted, which password protects it, and that a lost password means no one, including the developer, can open or recover it (section 15).
    • Reminders: by default the app reminds you monthly with a banner on Today (Back up now or Remind me later). Change it to weekly, every 3 months or off under Backup reminder. "Last backup" shows when you last made one.

    15. Automatic backups

    Open Settings → Backups. Automatic backups are off until you turn them on. Every change to these settings is kept in the audit log (the password itself never is).

    Settings

    • Automatic backups on or off.
    • Frequency: every hour (default), every 2 hours, every 4 hours, or daily. If nothing changed since the last backup, that interval is skipped (a backup is still saved at least once a day).
    • Keep last: 24 backups by default (6 to 168). Older automatic backups in the folder are removed (rotated) together with their readable copies. Other files in the folder are never touched.
    • Choose backup location (or Change location): pick the folder once. The first backup is saved straight away.
    • Last backup shows the time, the folder, how many files and their size, and the status (OK, next backup time, or what went wrong).
    • Back up now saves a backup immediately.

    What protects each backup (shown under every backup button)

    Under Back up now, the reminder banner and Export, two or three short lines always say:

    • The encryption: for example Encrypted with AES-256 (.mblbackup); readable copies in an AES-256 password-protected .zip.
    • The password: your app passcode, or your app passcode for the .mblbackup and your separate backup password for the .zip.
    • If the password is lost, no one can open or recover the backup, including the developer. There is no reset and no copy anywhere.

    What's in each backup

    • Encrypted backup (.mblbackup), always included: every entry, note, photo and setting, encrypted with AES-256 (AES-GCM, key from your passcode with PBKDF2, 600,000 rounds). It opens only with the app passcode you had when it was made, through Import. It's the only file the app can restore.
    • Readable copies (off by default): tick PDF, Markdown (.md), Word (.docx) and/or Excel (.xlsx). They list your entries like a report (all dates). Notes are left out unless you tick Include notes in readable copies.

    Readable copies contain patient details (room, initials, chart/MRN, codes and, if ticked, notes). They are always put inside an AES-256 password-protected .zip. Once someone unzips them they are not encrypted, so save backups only to a secure location you control.

    The .zip password

    • Use my app passcode (default), or Use a separate backup password. A separate password needs at least 12 characters and passes a strength check. It is kept only inside the app's encrypted storage, never in plain text, and never in the audit log or the backup files.
    • A .zip password can be guessed much faster than your passcode can be attacked in a .mblbackup (zip files use a weaker key-stretching step). Use a long password, such as several words with numbers, especially if your passcode is short. The app warns you when it is.
    • Readable copies are always inside the AES-256 .zip; since version 9 there is no option to save them unencrypted.
    • Why not a password-protected PDF? The PDF library in the app can only make the older RC4 PDF encryption, not AES-256, so PDF copies go inside the AES-256 .zip instead.

    Opening the password-protected .zip

    • Windows: 7-Zip (free) or WinRAR. File Explorer's built-in zip support can't open AES-256 zips.
    • Mac: The Unarchiver or Keka (both free), or Terminal: tar -xf FILE.zip --passphrase PASSWORD (or 7zz x FILE.zip if 7-Zip is installed). Double-clicking in Finder (Archive Utility) and the unzip command can't open AES-256 zips.
    • iPhone / iPad: the Files app can't open AES-256 zips; use the free iZip app. Android: use an archive app that supports AES zip. Details in section 12.
    • The .mblbackup is opened only by this app (Settings → Encrypted backup → Import).

    How it works on each device

    • Chrome or Edge on a computer: choose a folder once (for example in Documents, or a synced OneDrive, Google Drive or iCloud Drive folder). While the app is open and unlocked, a backup is saved at your interval, and again when the app locks or the tab is closed (if the browser allows the time). After the browser restarts, it may ask you once to Allow access to the folder again; a banner on Today asks for it.
    • iPhone and iPad app: choose a folder in Files (On My iPhone, iCloud Drive or another provider). Android app: choose a folder on the phone or in a cloud drive such as Google Drive. Backups are saved while the app is open, and the app tries to finish one when it goes to the background. Phones don't allow guaranteed background runs: if the app stays closed, no backups happen until you open and unlock it again.
    • Safari, Firefox and other browsers without folder access (including the Home Screen web app on iPhone): the app can't save to a folder by itself. With automatic backups on, a Backup reminder banner appears on Today at your interval. Back up now opens the share sheet (Save to Files, AirDrop…) or downloads the files; Later hides it until the next interval. Old copies aren't removed for you here.
    • Never while locked: the key that decrypts your data only exists while the app is unlocked, so nothing runs while it's locked or closed. The backup started when it locks uses data already in memory and finishes in the background where possible.

    If a backup fails

    A red banner on Today says what went wrong (for example the folder was moved or deleted, permission is needed, or the drive is full), with Try again and Backup settings. It never interrupts what you're doing, and your entries stay safe on this device. The app tries again automatically (within 15 minutes, or at your interval). Failures and recoveries are kept in the audit log.

    16. Privacy, the law and your responsibility

    Exports contain health information

    An export contains health information. Keep it encrypted, and send the password separately, never in the same email or message. Store and send it only as permitted by Alberta's Health Information Act (HIA), your custodian's or AHS (Alberta Health Services) privacy and information-security policies, and the privacy law and privacy commissioner's guidance in your own province or territory (e.g. the Alberta OIPC). Delete copies you no longer need.

    Your responsibility

    You, as the custodian or as an affiliate of a custodian (for example AHS or your clinic), are responsible for collecting, using, storing, sending and disposing of this information in a compliant way. MedBilling Logs is designed to support compliance: data is encrypted on the device, every export is encrypted, and the app does not transmit exports or any data anywhere by itself. It is not certified or approved by AHS, the OIPC or any government body, and it is not legal advice. Check your custodian's policies, and contact your privacy officer, the CMPA or the OIPC if you are unsure.

    Tips

    • Record only what you need for billing. Add a name or MRN/PHN only if you need it.
    • Keep your phone's own screen lock on, and use a passcode or passphrase you don't use anywhere else.
    • Keep the auto-lock short, and tap Lock before handing your phone to anyone. The full-screen timer shows only the time, so it is safe for others to see.
    • Share reports only through secure, approved channels. Don't leave copies in Downloads or email.
    • This is a personal billing log, not the medical record. Chart every encounter in the patient's chart.
    • If your phone is lost or data may be exposed, tell the custodian (e.g. AHS or your clinic) and contact the CMPA right away. A breach may have to be reported under the HIA.
    • The app has no accounts, analytics or trackers, and it sends nothing anywhere. Files leave the device only when you share or download them.

    17. FAQ and troubleshooting

    I forgot my passcode or passphrase.
    It can't be recovered. Use Forgot passcode? to erase and start over, then import a backup if you remember its passcode.
    Face ID / fingerprint / passkey stopped working.
    Use your passcode or passphrase; it always works. Easy unlock turns off after a passcode change, after the device's enrolled faces or fingers change, or if the passkey was deleted. Turn it on again in Settings → Lock → Easy unlock.
    Can Face ID or a passkey open my exported files?
    No. Export passwords are always typed, must be different from your passcode, and are never stored.
    I don't see "Unlock with a passkey" in my browser.
    Your browser doesn't support the passkey PRF extension (it needs Safari 18 or later, current Chrome or Edge, or Firefox 148 or later on a computer). The option is hidden rather than offered in a weaker form.
    Does the timer stop when the app locks or the phone sleeps?
    No. Timers use clock times and keep counting. Unlock and tap Stop (or fix the end time) when you're done.
    I opened Fee Desk and the app locked. Did I lose my entry?
    No. An open entry is saved as an encrypted draft and reopens after you unlock.
    My entries are missing.
    Check you opened the same app you used before (on iPhone, the Home Screen app and Safari keep separate data), and that you're on the same device. If website data was cleared or the app was removed, restore from a backup.
    How do I move to a new phone?
    On the old phone, Export a backup. On the new phone, install the app, create a passcode, then Import the backup with the passcode it was made with.
    Why does an entry say "Entered later"?
    It was back-dated, time was added, or its times were edited. See section 7.
    It says the times overlap.
    Another entry covers the same time. Fix the times, or tap Save anyway if it's correct.
    Does it work without internet?
    Yes, after the first open. Only the Resources and Fee Desk links need a connection.
    The app says "Updating to the new version…"
    Wait a few seconds. If it says "Update ready", close the app completely and reopen it. Your data isn't affected.
    A diagnostic code isn't in the suggestions.
    Type it anyway; any code is accepted. Use Look up in Fee Desk to check it.
    Are the units and codes official?
    No. They're a guide. Confirm in the Alberta Schedule of Medical Benefits, or contact AMA billing support.
    The time period or holiday looks wrong.
    Periods follow America/Edmonton time and your list of periods. Check Settings → Time periods and holidays: switch a holiday on or off, or tick "Today is a statutory holiday". Always verify against the current SOMB.
    Why did a red timer prompt appear when I unlocked?
    A timer ran past the red limit (3 hours by default). Stop it now or at the real end time, or keep it running. Change the limits under Settings → Timers.
    The full-screen timer went away.
    Auto-lock still applies. Unlock and reopen it with ⛶, or set a longer auto-lock before a long procedure.
    I tapped Stop or Delete by mistake.
    Tap Undo within 5 seconds. After that, edit the entry (a corrected time is marked "entered later"), or find deleted entries under Settings → Audit log → Deleted entries.
    Can I use it on a computer or tablet?
    Yes. Open the same web address in Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox. On a tablet or computer, Today shows your controls on the left and the day's entries on the right. History becomes a table with end time, units, setting and facility, and time periods. Settings and Resources show in columns. Data stays in that browser on that device, encrypted. To move data between devices, use an encrypted backup (Settings → Encrypted backup).
    Can I add time to an entry, or start another encounter for the same patient?
    Yes. Open the entry (or tap ⋯ on its History row) and use + Add time or Same patient, new encounter. See section 5.
    Where did my old note go?
    It is now the first note in the entry's Notes list, with the time the entry was created.
    Why aren't my notes in the report?
    For privacy, notes are only included when you tick Include notes before tapping Create encrypted file.
    Why is my report a .zip?
    Every export is encrypted with AES-256 to protect patient information. Open it with iZip (iPhone/iPad), The Unarchiver or Keka (Mac) or 7-Zip (Windows) and the export password. See section 12.
    Why was my export password refused?
    The message under the password says why: too short, too predictable (common words, sequences, repeats), contains a patient's name or MRN/PHN, or is the same as your app passcode.
    How do I set up automatic backups?
    Settings → Backups: turn on Automatic backups, pick how often and how many to keep, then Choose backup location. In Safari you get an hourly reminder instead. See section 15.
    I can't open the backup .zip on my Mac or PC.
    It's AES-256 encrypted, which Finder and Windows File Explorer can't open. Use 7-Zip on Windows, or Keka / The Unarchiver on a Mac (or tar -xf FILE.zip --passphrase PASSWORD in Terminal), with the backup password shown under Back up now.
    I lost my backup password.
    No one can open those backups, including the developer. Set a new separate password (or switch back to the app passcode) and tap Back up now so new backups use it.
    A red "Automatic backup didn't work" banner appeared.
    Tap Backup settings and Change location to choose the folder again, or Allow access if the browser asks. Your entries are safe on the device in the meantime.
    Where can I get advice?
    CMPA (1-800-267-6522), AMA billing support, or the CPSA. Links are in the Resources tab.
